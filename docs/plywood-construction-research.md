# Birch-plywood construction evidence and one bounded atlas experiment

Research date: 2026-11-03, initially against `50c57a2`. The research itself downloaded no new assets or applications. Existing asset-license findings in `plywood-fidelity-research.md` were accepted; no broad asset search was repeated. The subsequent implementation below is an authored hybrid, not a documented 32 mm product or an edge scan.

The bounded search did not verify a standard manufacturer's exact 32 mm construction. This does not prove none exists. Do not relabel an invented 32 mm / 21-ply stack as a documented product. For a nominal 30 mm / 21-ply model, dividing nominal thickness gives 1.429 mm per ply including its share of bonds; dividing the specified actual interval gives 1.338–1.424 mm. These are calculated average pitches, **not individual veneer measurements**. The Riga 1.45 mm specification concerns its stated veneer construction and should not be silently treated as the final average of another brand's panel.

## Why the edge cannot be a single repeated face strip

The task-provided baseline is a 3.25 mm single-veneer repeat with broad identical dark seams. At 30–32 mm this represents approximately 9.2–9.8 repeat periods, rather than the documented 21 plies of the 30 mm target. That is a construction mismatch before any subjective realism judgment.

For a straight board edge, when the veneer grain axis lies along the edge the cut exposes longitudinal anatomy; when it points through the edge the cut exposes end grain. This is a geometric inference from the documented cross lay-up. The adjacent perpendicular edge swaps those roles. Along a rounded corner or bevel, the cut transitions through oblique orientations. Consequently, a random offset of the same longitudinal face photograph in every layer cannot recreate the missing end-grain anatomy. Nor should alternate layers be made uniformly dark: grain orientation and adhesive boundaries are separate signals.

Clear seal/varnish can retain wood color variation while its coating changes the specular surface. Roughness and normal amplitude depend on actual sanding and finish. Broad color darkening is not evidence of a depressed glue groove; a color photograph is not a height measurement. The sources above do not establish finish microstructure in micrometres or measured BRDF values. Numerical choices below are authored hypotheses requiring visual validation.

## One bounded full-stack experiment

Use existing local CC0 maple/fine-grained maps only. Call the result a **photo-derived hybrid edge atlas**, never a plywood scan. Use a small isolated 30 mm specimen first: 21 plies and 20 internal boundaries, matching the documented nominal construction. This avoids changing the frozen production board or pretending that its 32 mm height is a catalog product.

1. Author a 2048 × 512 atlas representing an assumed 160 × 30 mm rectangular edge region (12.8 px/mm horizontally, 17.07 px/mm vertically). Use 21 distinct bands averaging 1.429 mm pitch. Constrain optional slight width changes so total height stays 30 mm; this variation is authored, not a measurement. Face plies may differ after sanding, but no exact sanding deduction is known.
2. Allocate separate atlas regions/maps for the two edge orientations. Use independently selected, low-contrast maple image strips for longitudinal detail. For alternate end-grain bands author restrained diffuse-porous mottling with no claim of recovered anatomy. Fine-grained imagery may supply weak irregularity after rejecting dirty/worn marks. Keep layer identity consistent where the two edge charts meet. Use perimeter-distance U and normalized full-thickness V, not a repeated single-ply V; map corner transitions explicitly.
3. Make bond masks separate from wood. Begin with assumed 0.05–0.10 mm visual bond widths (0.85–1.71 pixels at this atlas height), comparing an antialiased narrow-line variant against the baseline. This is an experimental range, **not source-backed cured bond thickness**. Avoid increasing seam width merely to survive denoising. Use brown rather than universal pure black and vary subtly, with an unvaried control to judge whether variation adds value.
4. Bake independent sRGB albedo, linear roughness, and tangent-space OpenGL normal maps. Start the normal control flat; compare one shallow authored relief variant bounded to ±0.01 mm, explicitly unmeasured. Keep glue pigment separate from relief. A sealed-edge roughness control can start at 0.4 with variation ±0.03, also unmeasured; these are renderer settings, not physical gloss measurements. Preserve identical physical sampling and UV transforms among maps.
5. In a scratch preview only, compare baseline and atlas under the same fixed close camera, one grazing light, identical exposure/sampling, and both undenoised and production denoised output. Inspect visible layer count, uniform stripe repetition, anatomy distinction, corner continuity, seam aliasing, excessive relief, and whether detail persists at delivered resolution. A count/UV test verifies implementation, not realism. Require an actual side-by-side human visual improvement before proposing production changes; an unsuccessful prototype remains scratch work.

If maintaining a 32 mm board is essential later, label 21 plies stretched across 32 mm (1.524 mm average pitch) an authored approximation and review its appearance separately. Better construction fidelity would require selecting an actual 32 mm product or documenting a fabrication choice, not treating the 30 mm specification as proof.

Material Maker is optional, not necessary for this single baked trial. The [official MIT-licensed project](https://github.com/RodZill4/material-maker) can express reusable strip/mask graphs, but installing an authoring application or assuming a CLI adds no missing plywood anatomy. A small deterministic image-generation script using the existing licensed maps is sufficient for this bounded experiment. No Material Maker installation or CLI validation was performed.

## Subsequent render experiment and implementation

Three fixed-camera 29 mm specimens were rendered at 256 samples with both raw linear accumulation displayed through the normal output transform and guided OIDN: the former 3.25 mm repeating layer, a 21-layer hybrid, and a richer hybrid with independent boundary waviness and stronger borrowed grain. Both normal controls were flat to isolate albedo. The first hybrid reduced stripe thickness but remained too uniform. The richer variant reduced the painted-line appearance, while still lacking convincing cut anatomy. No blind comparison or photographic equivalence was established.

The richer candidate was then screened on the actual generated 30-file maze at its low view. Production retains the existing physical 32 mm board and uses 21 authored veneers across that height; it does **not** claim to reproduce the manufacturer construction above. Average pitch is therefore 1.524 mm before authored width variation. The material borrows rescaled face grain from the existing CC0 maple image, uses separate narrow brown bond masks, and independently authors shallow relief below 0.01 mm. Adjacent-edge grain parity and real end-grain anatomy remain approximations. Low-view stripe dominance is reduced; the whole scene still reads as a render.

`npm run bake:plywood` bakes the color and tangent-normal PNGs using the local input and the pure `plywood-finish.js` author. The manifest records source/output SHA256 hashes and byte counts; `npm run assets:3d` checks them without launching an authoring browser. Two local bakes produced identical PNG hashes; cross-platform PNG-encoder identity is not claimed. The full stack occupies V=0–1, with a perimeter-distance U chart using whole repeats to close its phase. Physical board positions, normals and draw groups match the former construction in the regression fixtures.

Texture readiness now precedes photo GPU allocation. Asset errors or a 15 s deadline produce valid fallback pixels; late images retain the fallback already packed by a photograph. Cancellation/rescan while awaiting images cannot revive an old photo. These are loading/representation checks, not a fidelity certificate. The [low-view material preview](maze-fidelity/plywood-stack-low.png) is an actual generated 256-sample/OIDN export, captured before the final boundary guard below.

The pinned tracer packs all maps into a repeat-wrapped array, ignoring each source's clamp mode. A real GPU border probe found a maximum 11-level difference between source-clamped and packed sampling at the stack boundaries. Shared half-texel V guards reduce this to 1 level, within the 8-bit quantization tolerance of the probe. This changes sampling at the atlas boundary, not the board's physical dimensions or layer count; it does not establish whole-image AOV equality.
