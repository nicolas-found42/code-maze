# Bead maze: focused 3D tooling research

Checked 2026-11-03 against upstream source. Recommendation: combine **N8AO contact shading**, **pmndrs/postprocessing**, and **a small library of genuinely bored, softened bead meshes**. Add CSG only to generate shapes that cannot be expressed cleanly with a lathe. This targets the toy's missing contact depth and manufactured edges without changing its plain JavaScript architecture.

## Discovery provenance

[AxiomeCG/awesome-threejs](https://github.com/AxiomeCG/awesome-threejs#tools) lists three-mesh-bvh and three-bvh-csg, plus the React and Svelte postprocessing wrappers; its resources also list ambientCG PBR textures. Following those projects leads to the vanilla postprocessing library and its N8AO integration. [Pico's awesome-webxr-development](https://github.com/Pico-Developer/awesome-webxr-development) independently lists realism-effects. An awesome listing is discovery evidence, not proof of performance or adoption.

## Exact mashup for this repository

At the research baseline, `docs/vendor/three.module.js` declared revision 160 and `package.json` did not manage Three. `docs/bead-scene.js` used `window.THREE`, event-driven rendering, physical clearcoat materials, a JPEG environment, and transparent materials for selection. Integrations must share the **same** Three module instance rather than silently bundle a second version.

1. Keep the existing lathe route for rings and spherical beads, but close the profile with a continuous inner bore wall and soften the bore lip. For a rounded cube or unusual prism, generate a watertight rounded solid, subtract a slightly overlong bore using CSG, and cache one geometry. CSG is optional when analytic geometry already gives the desired shape.
2. Preserve physically meaningful scale: rails have radius 4.8 and existing bores radius 7. Start AO radius around 12–24 local units and tune by screenshot comparison. This is a proposed scene-specific starting point, not a benchmark result.
3. Use `RenderPass → N8AOPostPass → final antialias/output stage` with the pmndrs composer. Upstream explicitly distinguishes `N8AOPostPass` (requires a preceding RenderPass) from `N8AOPass` (replaces the Three addon RenderPass). See [N8AO usage](https://github.com/N8python/n8ao#usage).
4. Preserve one color/tone-mapping conversion. N8AO documents gamma-correction interactions; verify painted colors against the unprocessed renderer. Avoid stacking the existing ACES exposure with an additional unplanned tone mapper.
5. Leave N8AO accumulation disabled while retaining render-on-demand. Its [accumulation notes](https://github.com/N8python/n8ao#accumulation) warn that object motion is not detected. Its [transparency notes](https://github.com/N8python/n8ao#transparency) explain the extra render of transparent objects and possible artifacts with depth writes; explicitly test faded unselected beads and the halo.
6. Pair shading with restrained roughness variation, correct grain direction and studio reflections. AmbientCG was discovered through the awesome list, but new external texture downloads should be chosen only after licensing and asset size checks for the selected files. More postprocessing cannot repair a visibly wrong bead silhouette.

Before shipping, compare fixed-camera screenshots with AO on/off; verify bore silhouettes, rail contact, selection transparency, readable labels, resize and zoom. Benchmark a large graph on an integrated GPU. Keep the existing canvas fallback and use direct rendering if effect initialization fails. No performance claims are made here: these recommendations have not themselves been benchmarked in this repo.

## Implemented and measured

The shipped combination now uses N8AO 2.0.1, postprocessing 6.35.3, CSG 0.0.16 and BVH 0.8.3. A pinned authoring dependency on Three 0.160.0 matches the existing browser core. Five bored bead shapes share geometry; the rounded block is Boolean-baked. The review also found inward-facing lathe shells, corrected their winding, and independently tested exterior triangles and drill-axis rays. Studio PMREM reflections, timber bore materials, subtle paint structure, ash normal/roughness maps and world-scaled veneer UVs accompany the renderer changes.

Neural-Medium is used on stages at least 700 CSS pixels wide, Low/Poisson below that. `npm run test:fidelity` exercises selection, responsive quality changes, missing optional assets, repeated rescans, runtime compositor failure/disposal and 600 nodes. One measured run on Chrome 156 / ANGLE Metal / Apple M5 Pro recorded synchronized orbit frames of 25, 14 and 13 ms for 600 nodes, with stable tracked GPU allocations across three rescans. These are local observations, not a cross-device benchmark or a guarantee of mobile performance. The [before](maze-fidelity/before.jpg) and [after](maze-fidelity/after.jpg) close views use the same 30-file fixture and camera inputs. Geometry/material improvements and shading both change those images; the pair does not isolate AO's contribution. The script additionally captures an identical-material/camera AO-off view in `.scratch/verification/fidelity/ao-off.jpg` for isolated inspection. Fitted camera depth bounds and explicit AO bias remove the self-occlusion stipple observed during integration.

Rebuild details and license copies are [recorded alongside the assets](vendor/fidelity-libraries.md). This is a more detailed procedural toy, with visible model and contact improvements; no photographic-indistinguishability claim is made.

This pass distinguishes the interactive graph renderer from the separate Blender illustration. ### Highest-impact renderer experiment: progressive tracing of the actual graph

The previous latest-version incompatibility finding does **not** rule out an older release. [three-gpu-pathtracer v0.0.23 manifest](https://github.com/gkjohnson/three-gpu-pathtracer/blob/v0.0.23/package.json) declares Three >=0.151.0, BVH >=0.7.4 and xatlas-web ^0.1.0. Three r160 and this repository's BVH 0.8.3 satisfy those declared ranges. Its development baseline is r163, however, and source inspection exposes compatibility defects; peer ranges alone are insufficient verification. Pin an audited old release rather than importing current main, whose renderer has moved to WebGPU. [Current project](https://github.com/gkjohnson/three-gpu-pathtracer).

The [versioned API](https://github.com/gkjohnson/three-gpu-pathtracer/tree/v0.0.23#webglpathtracer) already exports `WebGLPathTracer`: build from the real scene/camera once, then call `renderSample()` repeatedly at rest. Use `updateCamera()` on orbit/resize, `updateMaterials()` for selection opacity changes, and rebuild after graph geometry changes. Its raster fallback, delay, minimum samples and fade controls support a direct-render-during-interaction design. Keep the current N8AO route available for unsupported devices. Do not feed N8AO's darkened image into the tracer: trace scene geometry/materials and present its own final image. The same API documents WebGL2, material and instancing limitations; check selection overlays separately and preserve picking against the original objects.

**Audit before integration:** [v0.0.23 WebGLPathTracer source](https://github.com/gkjohnson/three-gpu-pathtracer/blob/v0.0.23/src/core/WebGLPathTracer.js) accesses `scene.backgroundRotation` and `scene.environmentRotation` without guards. r160 lacks those newer scene fields, and the constructor internally creates its own Scene, so adding fields only to the application scene is insufficient. Vendor a minimal documented fallback or select another source-audited version. The actual control name is `filterGlossyFactor`, despite the README's different spelling. Its `dispose()` references `_renderQuad`, whereas construction creates `_quad`, and omits other owned resources: audit/fix cleanup before repeated rescans. These findings are source-level risks, not a completed runtime integration test.

The same source accepts equirectangular data and converts true CubeTextures; the existing PMREM CubeUV render-target texture is neither. Do not pass `pmrem.fromScene(...).texture` into the tracing environment loader. Capture the studio to a floating-point cube and convert it, use a floating-point equirectangular studio map, or use a procedural equirectangular DataTexture. Preserve an environment that the raster renderer can also interpret. [Environment data preprocessing](https://github.com/gkjohnson/three-gpu-pathtracer/blob/v0.0.23/src/uniforms/EquirectHdrInfoUniform.js) directly expects pixel arrays and builds lighting importance tables; a JPEG image or arbitrary GPU render-target texture needs conversion/readback first.

### Light shape and materials before another screen-space effect

The live scene currently combines a hemisphere fill, directional lights and a RoomEnvironment PMREM; the Blender still uses large disk area lights and true multi-bounce Cycles. This is a concrete lighting-model gap. The [versioned area-light example](https://github.com/gkjohnson/three-gpu-pathtracer/blob/v0.0.23/example/areaLight.js) demonstrates `ShapedAreaLight`, HDR equirectangular lighting, MIS and controls-driven camera resets. Start with a broad rectangular key, weaker fill and strip rim, tuned to real toy scale. Their size should shape paint reflections and soft contact shadows. The browser's standard [RectAreaLight](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/RectAreaLight.html) supplies rectangular PBR illumination but has no raster shadow support, so simply replacing the directional key does not reproduce area-light shadows. Keep a suitable shadow-casting raster light for the interaction preview.

The current paint reuses one small procedural texture for both height and roughness; wire paint is spatially uniform. A better authoring step is separate correlated height, roughness and coat-normal maps, with grain direction following each bead and slight deterministic per-object variation. Add small manufacturing variation near the bore lip and selected paint edges only where visible. This is an art-direction proposal, not a claim that random damage automatically improves realism. Geometry still controls the bore silhouette and rounded edge highlights; added noise cannot repair those.

[Awesome-Blender's material tools](https://github.com/bgteach/Awesome-Blender#texturematerial-) lead to [Material Maker](https://www.materialmaker.org/), whose first-party documentation describes procedural PBR graphs and texture export. Use it offline to author reusable toy-paint/plywood maps; no browser runtime dependency is needed. The same [awesome list's HDRI section](https://github.com/bgteach/Awesome-Blender#hdris-) leads to Poly Haven. Its role is a physically useful studio lighting source, not a substitute picture of the maze. Select and pin a particular asset, retain its license and test the download size before integration. An awesome-list mention establishes discovery only.

### Correct the existing Cycles reference before using it as a target

The Blender script presently requests a bore radius equal to `WIRE_R` (0.0042 m), then lowers each bead by 0.0012 m. That causes rail/bore overlap rather than a mechanically plausible resting clearance. It also assigns painted material to every face after boring, erasing the exposed timber bore, while the live scene already has a separate bore material. Its `cylinder` bead kind falls through to the sphere branch. Correct those scene-specific issues and compare the same generated graph before treating the still as a reference for the browser.

The wood shader box-projects tangent-space normal images and then adds two output normal vectors and normalizes them. Audit that mapping rather than assuming two normal vectors can be combined like colors. A practical alternative is a consistent UV normal map with a secondary scalar scratch height passed through the Normal input of a Bump node; Blender explicitly provides that input for chaining. [Blender Bump node manual](https://docs.blender.org/manual/id/4.4/render/shader_nodes/vector/bump.html). The script already uses 768 samples, denoising and several diffuse/glossy bounces, so raising sample counts before fixing intersecting geometry and material mapping is unlikely to be the best next experiment.

Use one actual scanned graph and identical camera/geometry for raster and settled traced captures. Inspect bore contact, colored indirect light under beads, paint highlight width, wood grain scale and soft base/table shadows at both overview and close crop. ### Integration follow-up: Apple clearcoat failure — 2026-11-03

During integration, the parent implementation agent reported that v0.0.23 rendered every surface with nonzero clearcoat black on Chrome 156 / ANGLE Metal / Apple M5 Pro; disabling clearcoat restored those surfaces. This is a reported local observation, not a reproduction performed by this research pass.

A direct upstream counterpart exists: [issue #711](https://github.com/gkjohnson/three-gpu-pathtracer/issues/711) reports black clearcoat with v0.0.23 on an M4 Mac, including a material-ball reproduction. The maintainer closed it because WebGL work was being retired, without providing a correction. The related [issue #682](https://github.com/gkjohnson/three-gpu-pathtracer/issues/682) records black transmission on Apple devices; the maintainer also reproduced it in Chrome and suspected shader translation/backend behavior. Neither closed status establishes a fix.

Compared the installed v0.0.23 source with official v0.0.24, v0.0.25 and v0.0.26: the clearcoat evaluation/sampling, lobe weights and GGX functions are unchanged. In the inspected surface-record function, later versions change alpha-map UV transformation, not coat setup. A version bump by itself therefore has no source-backed reason to repair this specific failure. [v0.0.26 BSDF](https://github.com/gkjohnson/three-gpu-pathtracer/blob/v0.0.26/src/shader/bsdf/bsdf_functions.glsl.js), [v0.0.26 GGX](https://github.com/gkjohnson/three-gpu-pathtracer/blob/v0.0.26/src/shader/bsdf/ggx_functions.glsl.js).

A bounded diagnostic sequence that preserves the intended material model:

1. Use a single smooth sphere, plain physical material, constant environment and nonzero coat; reproduce the upstream material-ball condition independently of graph merging.
2. Temporarily retain coat Fresnel attenuation while replacing only the coat-added BRDF/PDF with finite constants. If color returns, restore the terms separately to locate the failing computation.
3. Refactor coat evaluation to return independent coat color/PDF outputs, then compose the base attenuation and coat contribution in `bsdfEval`. This avoids the nested `inout vec3 color` update and is a shader-compiler workaround hypothesis, not a proven upstream correction.
4. Guard coat evaluation against zero/grazing denominators and invalid directions. Its current entry test accepts `clearcoatWi.z == 0`; require positive incoming/outgoing coat cosines and half-vector dot products. The shared GGX code uses unbounded `acos` inputs and divisions by directional Z, so finite clamps and `acosSafe` are additional numerical-hardening candidates. Verify with nonzero-coat images and finite sample output rather than treating a successful compile as sufficient.

The integration agent also found that the scene merger flattened material arrays while retaining one mesh group, shifting material IDs. It reports splitting material groups into single-material submeshes as its repair. Keep this separate from the coat issue: a minimal single-material sphere eliminates the merger as a confounder. Preserve painted exteriors and bare timber bores when checking the repaired graph.

### Implementable denoising and microtexture route — 2026-11-03

**Keep the generated scene and replace only the final noise filter.** Direct inspection of `bead-photograph.js` finds a color-only `DenoiseMaterial` applied in `renderToCanvasCallback`, with 256 accumulated samples; `bead-materials.js` derives both paint roughness and normals from the same 256px height signal. Those are specific seams for an experiment, independent of the known Apple clearcoat defect.

The specialist [pissang/oidn-webgpu](https://github.com/pissang/oidn-webgpu) is a concrete option: its observed [manifest](https://github.com/pissang/oidn-webgpu/blob/main/package.json) says **0.6.0**, and its README records the rename from `oidn-web` (old name remains through 0.5.0). It uses WGSL rather than a WASM CPU fallback. The [browser contract](https://github.com/pissang/oidn-webgpu/blob/main/docs/web.md) accepts color-only linear HDR as `{ data: Float32Array, width, height }`, RGBA/top-row-first, and returns floating-point HDR. Initialize `initUNetFromURL('/weights/rt_hdr.tza', undefined, { hdr: true })`, then call `tileExecute({ color, done, error })`. The returned function aborts pending work; `unet.dispose()` releases its resources. No Three version is required by the observed manifest, so this CPU-image boundary avoids upgrading r160 or replacing the geometry. WebGPU remains required; keep the current filter when unavailable. These APIs were source-audited, not executed in this repository.

For the existing WebGL tracer, copy/read its **unfiltered linear accumulation target**, flip bottom-up readback rows, expand/convert to float RGBA as necessary, and send that snapshot to OIDN. Upload the result as a linear float texture and apply the existing output tone mapping once. Do this after a bounded checkpoint or final accumulation, not every frame. Keep generation/camera tokens, abort on movement/close/rescan, and discard stale completions. Retain the raw accumulation for refinement; never accumulate denoised pixels. An initial lower-cost LDR experiment can denoise an actual rendered canvas using `rt_ldr.tza`/ImageData, but it processes already tone-mapped detail; the HDR path is the more defensible final route. Neither route invents a replacement toy or repairs incorrect lighting/materials.

The first-party [weights inventory](https://api.github.com/repos/pissang/oidn-webgpu/contents/weights) reports **1,830,792 bytes** for each color-only `rt_hdr.tza`/`rt_ldr.tza`, **1,841,160 bytes** for guided `rt_hdr_alb_nrm.tza`, **641,480 bytes** for guided small, and **7,698,439 bytes** for guided large. These are model files, not total runtime/GPU memory. Start color-only because this renderer currently produces no matching albedo/normal guide buffers. Guided models require both images at identical dimensions; the browser wrapper encodes normal XYZ into [0,1], unlike native OIDN's signed convention. Add real guide passes only if a fixed-camera test shows excessive grain/paint-detail loss. The weights are Apache-2.0; wrapper code is MIT. Vendor the selected file/license with a pinned source revision and verify its byte count/hash when integrating.

A second maintained specialist option is [pmndrs/denoiser](https://github.com/pmndrs/denoiser), observed manifest **2.0.0**, with `Denoiser.create()` and image-in/ImageData-out support. Its Three TSL path has optional peer Three >=0.185, so that integration is unsuitable for this r160 scene. Its standalone image boundary is still plausible, but its upstream README labels API changes alpha and validation Apple-only; avoid importing an old ONNX/WASM tutorial against the current default WGSL/AutoRuntime API. Prefer the smaller audited contract above for this bounded experiment, and measure readback plus inference rather than quoting another project's kernel-only timings.

For separate, controlled material maps, [Material Maker 1.4](https://github.com/RodZill4/material-maker/releases/tag/1.4) is an offline tool rather than a browser dependency. The [release API](https://api.github.com/repos/RodZill4/material-maker/releases/tags/1.4) dates it to **2025-10-05**, with a **112,695,147-byte macOS DMG** (Linux archive 87,918,599 bytes). Thus this is an established specialist route, not a newly released 2026 feature. Its documented [CLI](https://rodzill4.github.io/material-maker/doc/command_line.html) exports `.ptex` graphs with `--export-material --target Blender -o <output_path> <input_files>`. The [Blender export contract](https://rodzill4.github.io/material-maker/doc/export.html) emits PNG/EXR images and requires material wiring; it does not export this generated graph scene.

Proposed authoring experiment: store deterministic paint/wood `.ptex` graphs; export separate height-derived OpenGL normal and roughness maps at 512px or 1k, with a named physical repeat size matching bead UVs. Use shared low-frequency structure but independent fine roughness, rather than copying height wholesale into roughness. Keep normals/roughness linear and only base color sRGB. Calibrate paint highlight width and normal amplitude against the same fixed camera/light before adding edge wear. The exported compressed asset sizes depend on the graph and remain unmeasured; do not treat the tool download size as browser payload. This is an implementable asset pipeline, not evidence that arbitrary noise is physically measured enamel.

The [npm 0.6.0 registry record](https://registry.npmjs.org/oidn-webgpu/0.6.0) independently confirms the published release, ESM `lib/main.js` entry, sole runtime dependency `@petamoriken/float16 ^3.8.7`, and **1,331,107 unpacked package bytes** across 95 files; this is not its final browser bundle size. Ordinary WGSL does not require experimental GPU flags. The browser guide makes `shader-f16` optional and defaults to FP32 when absent; unsafe flags apply only to the experimental subgroup-matrix kernel. CPU HDR output alpha is forced to 1. `prepareForImage(width,height)` can warm shape allocations; the returned abort waits only for the current GPU tile, then suppresses callbacks. Explicitly cancel while hidden, because animation-frame scheduling itself resumes after a 100ms fallback. These lifetime details matter alongside the existing generation guard.

### Guided denoising follow-up — 2026-11-03

If color-only denoising erases the manufactured paint texture, test actual feature buffers. The pinned upstream revision **`aee3d609e1d45eb14da7c3010bb9aa4bd9988d6c`** contains [`rt_hdr_alb_nrm.tza`](https://github.com/pissang/oidn-webgpu/blob/aee3d609e1d45eb14da7c3010bb9aa4bd9988d6c/weights/rt_hdr_alb_nrm.tza): **1,841,160 bytes**, downloaded SHA-256 **`e586ef2ff48d7fbb7611986405220ed8fc5c13ca79bfc40be4dc742fbf959e1a`**. Retain its pinned [Apache-2.0 license](https://github.com/pissang/oidn-webgpu/blob/aee3d609e1d45eb14da7c3010bb9aa4bd9988d6c/weights/LICENSE.txt). Initialize with `{ hdr: true, aux: true }`; pass `color`, `albedo`, and `normal`, each `{ data: Float32Array, width, height }`, to `tileExecute`.

The decisive [CPU implementation](https://github.com/pissang/oidn-webgpu/blob/aee3d609e1d45eb14da7c3010bb9aa4bd9988d6c/src/UNet.ts) is `_processImageData`: dimensions must match, F32 guide values are divided by **1**, and byte guides by **255**. It copies RGB from each RGBA pixel; there is no guide sRGB decoding or coordinate transform. All images follow the [browser top-row-first contract](https://github.com/pissang/oidn-webgpu/blob/aee3d609e1d45eb14da7c3010bb9aa4bd9988d6c/docs/web.md). Consequently, capture linear albedo in [0,1], disable output tone mapping/color encoding for guide passes, and flip WebGL readback rows consistently for all three images.

**Do not decode MeshNormalMaterial output to signed normals before passing it to this browser wrapper.** Its [preprocessing source](https://github.com/pissang/oidn-webgpu/blob/aee3d609e1d45eb14da7c3010bb9aa4bd9988d6c/src/process.ts) explicitly keeps `n * 0.5 + 0.5` RGB and clamps it to [0,1]. Native OIDN uses a different signed convention. The [native OIDN feature guidance](https://www.openimagedenoise.org/documentation.html#normals) permits view/world shading normals, recommends including normal maps, and asks for matching reconstruction filters to avoid edge artifacts. It describes albedo as illumination-independent surface color and permits ignoring a nonabsorbing coat.

A same-camera raster guide pass is therefore a practical experiment for opaque painted toy surfaces: MeshBasicMaterial carrying each original color/map for albedo, MeshNormalMaterial carrying each original normalMap/normalScale for view-space shading normals, preserving all generated geometry and material groups. This is a proposed approximation, not an exact tracer AOV: single-sample raster guides differ from accumulated antialiasing and finite-aperture beauty. Use matched antialiasing or supersampled guides, and check silhouettes, bores and highlight microtexture. If depth of field matters, accumulate guides with the same lens jitter or reduce that mismatch before calling the result correct. A flat override normal material that drops the original normalMap would discard precisely the paint-detail guide this experiment needs.

## Photographed studio illumination — evaluated, not adopted

Primary-source research checked [Poly Haven Studio Small 09](https://polyhaven.com/a/studio_small_09), a photographed octabox/infinity-cyc environment. Its [files API](https://api.polyhaven.com/files/studio_small_09) declares a 6,312,947-byte 2K HDR; the downloaded file matched MD5 `b056fea247bc84b81d2e0987d44c87d8` and SHA256 `36724313c0fc66dab126ff90f081b85a0b1ef47be65eda1417bd20b033f0573f`. Poly Haven's [asset license](https://polyhaven.com/license) is CC0. The candidate remains outside the repository; no unused HDR download was added to the browser payload.

The installed [r160 RGBELoader](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r160/examples/jsm/loaders/RGBELoader.js) decodes the original Radiance RGBE to linear floating pixels. Those can feed the pinned pathtracer's [EquirectHdrInfoUniform](https://github.com/gkjohnson/three-gpu-pathtracer/blob/v0.0.23/src/uniforms/EquirectHdrInfoUniform.js), retaining importance sampling and avoiding a PMREM CubeUV mismatch. Its decode peaked at RGB 550.149, within the half-float range. Loader compatibility alone does not establish a visual gain.

The surrounding sweep changes this proposal: its top is about 56° above the subject horizon, while the candidate's brightest 0.1% pixels mostly sit around 11–33° elevation. A decoded spherical-radiance integral found only 0.1913% above 56.31°; this is an image statistic, **not** a measured blocked-light percentage, and excludes stage bounce. Simply replacing the gradient would conceal most recorded key-light directions behind the opaque stage. Retain finite lights inside the sweep; test an open/finite rear stage and controlled HDRI/key-light comparisons before adoption. Disabling shadow casting on the sweep changes shadow/MIS visibility without removing BSDF-ray intersections, so it is not a physically justified default repair. No HDRI-driven fidelity improvement is claimed.

A subsequent fixed-camera experiment rendered the same 30-file close view at 256 samples with guided OIDN: HDRI-only plus a large flat floor (44.5 s), the current surrounding sweep (89.4 s), and a shortened sweep (71.4 s). The flat floor produced more directional reflections; both sweeps produced pale diffuse fill and weaker material separation.
